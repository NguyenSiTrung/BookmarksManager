# Product Guidelines

## Voice and Copy

Use plain, concise, specific language. Describe what an action does, what data it uses, and whether it can be undone. Avoid claims such as "magic," "100% private," or "best" that the product cannot substantiate.

## Privacy and Consent

Before asking for consent, name the exact recipient and destination, the fields sent, the trigger, and the purpose. Leave consent unchecked by default. Distinguish local processing from requests to a user-chosen provider, and never imply that configuring a key alone grants consent.

## AI Decisions

Show confidence and the consequence of a suggestion without presenting a model judgment as certain. Clearly label review, approval, rejection, and undo, especially for moves, merges, and restructuring.

## Interface Direction

Design for large bookmark libraries: fast scanning, a readable folder hierarchy, keyboard access, and accessible controls. Use a restrained, neutral visual system until a visual brand is chosen. Do not use provider logos or imply endorsement.

## Store Communication

Keep the listing, permission explanations, privacy policy, and in-product disclosures consistent with shipped behavior. State that offline features work without a key and optional AI requires the user's own provider configuration.
